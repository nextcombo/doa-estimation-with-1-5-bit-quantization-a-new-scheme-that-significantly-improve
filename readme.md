Introduction: Direction of Arrival (DOA) estimation is a core research direction in array signal processing. It has wide applications in radar, sonar, and wireless communication, among others. Large-scale MIMO antenna array systems offer the possibility to improve DOA estimation performance, but full digital implementation brings extremely high computational complexity. To address the hardware cost, power consumption, and real-time processing challenges of large-scale antenna arrays, low-bit ADCs have gained widespread application due to their low cost and simplicity.
Zero-threshold 1-bit quantizers, due to their simple hardware structure and lowest cost, have become the mainstream choice for low-bit quantization. Research based on Bussgang's theorem and the law of arcsine confirms that under the assumption of zero mean Gaussian, the results of 1-bit quantization can recover full-precision information, achieving certain DOA estimation performance. However, this approach loses power information, which is crucial in applications such as wireless sensor network positioning. Therefore, non-zero-threshold 1-bit quantizers and hybrid architectures combining 1-bit with high-resolution ADCs have been proposed to alleviate the quantization loss of pure 1-bit systems.
In the research on 1-bit quantized DOA estimation, the law of arcsine is the core theory. Scholars have optimized this law by introducing time-varying thresholds and extending it to complex elliptic distributions, reducing quantization errors. Meanwhile, 2-bit quantizers have been proposed to capture more information, but their covariance estimation requires iterative reconstruction and lacks closed-form solutions similar to the 1-bit law of arcsine, resulting in higher computational complexity; compression sense has also been adapted to 1-bit measurement, but it suffers from amplitude ambiguity and threshold sensitivity.
To address the issue of excessive quantization error in traditional 1-bit ADCs, this paper proposes a 1.5-bit quantization framework without significantly increasing storage and sampling speed costs. By using dual symmetry non-zero thresholds to divide the signal amplitude into three levels, it captures more information than 1-bit quantization and reduces one comparator compared to 2-bit quantization, lowering hardware complexity. Unlike the empirical analysis in previous conference papers, this paper rigorously derives the closed-form expressions for the 1.5-bit related estimators, laying a solid theoretical foundation for this method.
